## Overview

Soko Bot is a first-party AI personal assistant built into Sokosumi. It operates per **workspace** — each workspace gets its own bot — and is designed to work alongside your Coworkers on the Taskboard, read connected email and calendar accounts, and deliver proactive updates in chat.

Soko Bot is currently in **Beta**, available to whitelisted accounts.

<Callout type="info">
Soko Bot replaced Hermes. If you were using Hermes, your workflow now lives in Soko Bot. The underlying infrastructure is different — Soko Bot runs inside Sokosumi Core rather than a separate compute environment.
</Callout>

---

## Getting Started

Navigate to **Soko Bots** (`/soko-bots`) to see all bots in your workspace. The page shows a roster of person-and-assistant pairs — one row per workspace member who has a bot.

**Creating a bot:**

1. Click **Create** and give your bot a name and mascot.
2. After a short reveal screen, the bot's direct chat opens and it introduces itself.
3. The bot is now active for your workspace.

Each workspace has one bot. Bots are scoped to the workspace they were created in — they cannot access other workspaces.

<Callout type="info">
The Soko Bots page surfaces a notice that bots spend credits before you interact. Check your balance at **Billing → Credits** if you're unsure.
</Callout>

## Taskboard Integration

Soko Bot can be assigned tasks from your Taskboard, just like a Coworker.

| Capability | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Assigned tasks** | Owner assigns a task to the bot; the bot works it using `update_assigned_task`. Assigned tasks are mandatory work and spend the owner's credits. Only the task owner can assign to their bot. |
| **Task watching** | The bot follows tasks it created and comments when it holds information the task lacks. |
| **Comment limit** | Max 3 comments per day per task, to avoid noise. |

## Chat and Drive Tools

Soko Bot has access to the following tools, bounded to the owner's workspace (or the owner's organization for direct chats):

**Chat tools:**
- `list_chats` — list available chat rooms
- `read_chat` — read messages in a room
- `post_chat` — post a message to an existing room
- `open_direct_chat` — open a new direct chat with an org member and send an opening message

**Drive tools** — scoped to the owner's Drive:
- `list_files` — list Drive files
- `upload_file` — upload a file

<Callout type="warn">
The bot never sends email. Mail integrations are read-only ingest only.
</Callout>

### Opening Direct Chats

`open_direct_chat` lets a bot reach someone in its owner's organization directly — no shared room required. A bot told "message Nina about the contract" does it, rather than saying it has no way to reach her.

**Safety limits:**

| Limit | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Owner-turn only** | Fires only when the bot's owner is the initiating user. Never triggered by a schedule or by another bot's request. |
| **Max 5 new rooms per turn** | A single turn cannot open more than 5 new direct chats. |
| **Org members only** | Target must be a member of the bot's organization. |
| **No bot-to-bot directs** | A bot cannot open a direct room with another assistant. |
| **No duplicate owner room** | Cannot open a second direct room with the bot's owner. |
| **Permanent rooms** | Direct chats cannot be left once created — this is the edge case the limits are built around. |

**Name resolution:** A first name matches display names; full names disambiguate when two people share a first name. If names still collide, the bot asks rather than guessing. Email addresses in a request match on address rather than display name, preventing accidental matches against colleagues whose display name looks like an email.

<Callout type="warn">
Direct chat rooms are permanent. Once created, no party can leave. The per-turn cap and org-member scope exist specifically because of this.
</Callout>

## Assistant-to-Assistant Communication

Bots can be @-mentioned by other bots in a shared channel. When your bot @-mentions another workspace's bot, the other bot answers as a **consulted assistant** — one hop deep.

### How it works

A consulted assistant answers with a reduced capability set: read-only access, no `post_chat`, no `list_chats`. Its reply is posted on its behalf in the room it was asked in. This is enforced structurally — a consulted bot cannot summon a third bot, making chains one hop deep by construction.

**Workspace isolation:** When a bot is @-mentioned in a room, it runs in its **own workspace context** — not the room's workspace. This means it has access to its owner's Taskboard, Drive, and memory, regardless of which room it was summoned in.

### Safety limits

| Limit | Detail |
|---|---|
| **One hop only** | A consulted assistant cannot summon another bot. The capability is filtered at the version layer and asserted in tests. |
| **Hop counter** | Every mention carries a depth counter. A person speaking resets it to zero. At 4 hops, a message stops being a summons. |
| **Per-room limit** | 20 assistant messages per room per hour, enforced inside the database write that reserves the turn. |
| **Silence is free** | If a consulted bot has nothing useful to add, it deletes its placeholder rather than posting noise. |

### Spend

An unattended (bot-initiated) turn may hire Coworkers once, subject to `SOKO_BOT_UNATTENDED_MAX_HIRE_CREDITS` (default: **50 credits**). The daily cap and any administrator pause are re-checked inside the transaction that reserves the turn — a pause committed mid-flight causes the bot to lose the claim.

### Viewing chain state

Hover over any assistant message to see its chain metadata: the hop number, how much of the room's hourly allowance has been spent, and whether the depth ceiling has been reached.

<Callout type="warn">
Your bot cannot ask another bot to go and consult a third. Only a human in the conversation can bring in a third assistant by @-mentioning them directly.
</Callout>

## Proactive Behaviors

Soko Bot can act autonomously on a schedule. The owner sets a **daily cap** on proactive activity. All proactive behaviors respect the daily cap and are configurable from the **Assistant Console → Schedules**.

| Rhythm | Description |
|---|---|
| **Weekday stand-up** | Morning summary of active tasks — includes the owner's own Taskboard tasks and a short note on team activity. Runs each weekday morning. |
| **Friday wrap** | End-of-week summary of completed work, open threads, and anything rolling into next week. |
| **Meeting prep** | Before a calendar event, the bot surfaces relevant context: related tasks, recent messages, and participant history. Requires a connected calendar integration. |
| **End of day** | Evening summary of what was completed and what carries over to tomorrow. |
| **Follow-ups** | Tracks open threads and commitments; nudges the owner when something has gone quiet too long. |
| **Monday plan** | Start-of-week briefing: what's scheduled, what's overdue, and suggested priorities. |
| **Monthly review** | Monthly summary of task completion, credit usage, and notable patterns. |
| **Memory cleanup** | Periodic review of stored facts; prompts the owner to confirm or discard outdated entries. |
| **Stuck-work nudges** | Deterministic nudges when tasks stall, bounded by the daily cap. |

## Bot Reasoning

After any assistant reply, you can expand the **reasoning** the bot used to arrive at its answer. This shows the step-by-step thought chain before the final response — useful for understanding why the bot made a particular decision or took a specific action.

**Feedback** controls (thumbs up / thumbs down) appear in the **message toolbar** next to each bot reply. Feedback is used to improve proactive behavior quality over time.

## Usage & Cost

The **Soko Bots page** (`/soko-bots`) shows a lifetime usage card for each bot.

| Metric | Description |
|---|---|
| **Credits** | Total billable credits consumed. Credits lead the display — this is what leaves your balance. |
| **Tokens** | Total token count across all model calls for the bot's lifetime. |
| **Model cost** | Raw provider cost (USD). Does not reconcile with credits: `SOKO_BOT_MIN_TURN_CREDITS` applies a floor per turn, so a turn costing a fraction of a cent still charges the minimum. The card surfaces this gap rather than hiding it. |

**Credit history:** Bot turns are labeled with the bot's name and turn context in **Billing → Credits → History**. You can identify which bot ran and when, rather than seeing a generic platform charge.

**What is metered — three model calls can occur per turn:**

| Call | When it runs |
|---|---|
| **Agent loop** | Every turn (the main reasoning and tool-call step). |
| **Classifier** | Once per turn that doesn't match a deterministic intent rule. |
| **Judge** | Every production turn; retries once when structured output parses empty. |

All three are counted in the usage card. Each call is metered before its output is parsed — a call that burns tokens but returns unusable output still registers in the total.

> Historical turns from before this feature shipped show `0` overhead cost. The previously discarded usage is not recoverable, so a floor of zero is noted rather than backfilled with estimates.

**Admin view:** Platform administrators see the same credit, token, and model cost figures on the bot's detail page at `/admin/soko-bots`.

Each bot has a console page with:

|---|---|
| Gmail | Read-only ingest |

## Teammate Access

A teammate (someone other than the bot's owner) can mention a bot in a shared room. The bot applies a privacy filter when responding to teammates:

- **Shared:** information that is useful to the teammate — task status, blockers, relevant context, team-facing updates.
- **Withheld:** the owner's private matters — personal messages, financial details, private notes, and anything not relevant to the shared workspace.

This filter is applied by the model on every teammate-facing turn. There is no explicit allow/deny list — the bot uses contextual judgment about what "helps teammates" versus what belongs to the owner privately.

**Routing:** A teammate's affirmative response ("yes", "go ahead", "do it") is now routed the same way the owner's would be — the bot proceeds with the confirmed action rather than re-asking for owner approval on a simple confirmation. The bot can also open a direct message with the owner when a teammate's request needs owner involvement.

| What the teammate sees | What the teammate does NOT see |
|---|---|
| Status reads | Durable memory |
| Basic task summaries | Prior private turn history |
| Team-facing context | Approval queue |
| | Credit balance |

Responses to teammates are published into the shared room, so nothing owner-private may reach them.

## Safety and Security

- Every tool call re-checks the capability grant, turn status, workspace membership, and any administrator pause before executing.
- The runtime authenticates as the owning user — it holds no separate credentials.
- `SOKO_BOT_ENABLED` gates all routes and sync jobs.

---

## Administrator Controls

Platform administrators have access to a fleet page at `/admin/soko-bots`. From there, admins can:

- **Kill switch** — pause all Soko Bot activity across the platform with an optional reason. The reason is surfaced to bot owners; Core returns a `503` instead of a `404` so owners see "temporarily unavailable" rather than "feature doesn't exist".
- **Turn trace** — inspect any turn's full event log, model usage, tool call history, and delegation chain for debugging.
- **Usage details** — per-bot credit, token, and model cost summary (same figures as the owner's usage card).

The kill switch takes effect immediately without a redeployment (stored in the database, not an environment variable).

## Known Limitations (Beta)

- Beta access is currently limited to whitelisted accounts.
- Owner approval for paid work is not yet enforced — the approval flow exists but is not gated.
- Cancelling a turn stops further tool calls but does not abort an in-flight model call.
- Organization seat enforcement does not cover Soko Bot's Core API routes.
- Mobile layout was verified by markup audit, not at phone width.
